## Findings (ticket 10)

All rows use the dictionary (Gurjeet, Priya, Rohan, Supabase, Vercel, Next.js, WhisperKit, MLX Swift, Qwen,
Figma). Cloud rows use the user's own keys; latency includes the network round trip from India.

**Speech to text, Hinglish mode.** Deepgram Nova-3 (`language=multi`) beats the local Hinglish model on
what it hears: **12.6 vs 16.4** agnostic WER, English items **9.7 vs 14.1**, median **0.71 s vs 0.89 s**.
It gets the English names and terms the local model can't: Vercel (local: "WhatsApp"), Supabase ("super
piece"), Qwen ("Coen"), invoice ("inverse"), finance team ("finest").
Its *strict* WER is worse (22.2 vs 20.1) because it writes Hindi in Devanagari, which our transliterator
romanises less naturally than the Hinglish model's own spelling: "cahie" for chahiye, "hum" for hoon
(chandrabindu), "agale" for agle. Improving the transliteration rules would close most of that gap.

**Speech to text, English mode.** Deepgram `en` is behind local turbo on English (11.9 vs 6.6) and, like
turbo, translates the Hindi clips; English mode is for English speakers.

**AI cleanup.** WER is unchanged by any cleanup (it only touches fillers and punctuation), so the
comparison is speed and how often the guard has to reject a rewrite:

| Cleanup | Median s (whole dictation) | Rewrites rejected (of 28) |
|---|---|---|
| Thorough, local Qwen3-4B | 2.37 | — |
| Cloud: Groq `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` (new default) | 1.08 | 4 |
| Cloud: OpenAI `gpt-4.1-mini` | 1.80 | 7 |

Groq's Qwen cleans in 0.16-0.29 s per transcript. The previous Groq default (`llama-3.3-70b-versatile`)
no longer exists; `openai/gpt-oss-20b` took 0.6-1.2 s.

**OpenAI Luna** (`gpt-5.6-luna`, `gpt-6-luna`, tried on request): 1.6-2.1 s per cleanup with default
reasoning, and 3 of 8 hit the 5 s timeout; with `reasoning_effort: none`, 1.2-1.3 s, and `gpt-5.6-luna`
then answered in Devanagari. Too slow for per-dictation cleanup next to Groq.
